Open Heavy Machinery · pre-alpha · Apache-2.0

Machines that answer back.

To your firmware, the world is frames on a bus and edges on its pins. We build the machine on the other side, in the open: a heavy-duty diesel you can talk to over J1939 today, and the core that will let any microcontroller face a crane, a robot or anything else it will never meet on a desk.

// how it started

Two engines that did not fit in the apartment.

I wanted to practise writing firmware for critical infrastructure the way it is really written: an STM32G431KB as the engine ECU of a Liebherr D936 and of a Mitsui MAN B&W 9S50ME, using every peripheral I wanted, against engines that answer back. Neither engine fits in an 8 m² apartment in Tokyo.

So I wrote the engine instead. open-crank is a heavy-duty diesel that speaks J1939, with an engine ECU that has opinions and a plant that never cheats. Writing it meant writing everything around it: a clock that never reads the wall, scenarios as data, a bench that grades firmware by its frames, tests that can fail.

None of that cares whether the machine is a crane, a robot with four motors or a set of train doors. To the microcontroller it is all voltages on pins and frames on a bus. That part became open-tick, the core every project here is built on.

The engines still do not fit in the apartment. The G431 no longer needs them to.

— deivideich, founder

// the family

One core, and the machines that run on it.

Each machine is its own project with its own physics and protocols. What they share lives in open-tick, and nothing is moved there until a second machine needs it. open-crank is the priority. open-tick grows only when open-crank needs it; sibling applications such as open-robots come later.

// how it fits

Your microcontroller cannot see the machine. It sees signals.

Applications bring the physics and the protocol. open-tick keeps time, plays scenarios and carries the signals to your board through a bridge. Today that is a CAN bus; next, the pins themselves.

Swipe the diagram sideways to see all of it.

On the bus today

Your firmware talks to the machine over CAN, exactly as a crane or vessel controller talks to its engine. vcan on a laptop, or a USB-CAN adapter and a real board.

On the pins planned

The bridge turns the plant's state into edges your timers and interrupts count: crank and cam wheels on a Tang Nano 20K first, encoders on a Pico when open-robots starts. The plant stays on a CPU; the bridge keeps the timing.

How faithful it is planned

Each machine states what it needs from a bridge, each bridge states what it guarantees, and a run that cannot be faithful is refused instead of passing quietly.

// direction

One finishable step at a time, in each project.

No dates. A milestone is done when its exit criteria are met. The full plans, with their criteria, live in each repository.

open-tick ROADMAP.md

  1. v0.1Extraction from open-crankdone

    Time, scenarios, traces and CAN bridges as a library; open-crank runs on it with byte-identical traces.

  2. v0.2Oracle

    Grade any controller only by what its transports show, proven first in open-crank's acceptance suite. Pulled by open-crank v0.2.1.

  3. v0.3Transport faults

    Lost, corrupted and delayed frames, bus-off and jitter, for every application. Pulled by open-crank v0.3.

  4. v0.4Tang Nano 20K bridge

    Crank and cam wheels, injector capture and analog outputs, the bridge as the clock, and the first fidelity contract. Pulled by open-crank v0.4.

  5. v0.5Profiles

    Machines described in reviewable YAML, compiled to C. Parameters and wiring, never equations. Pulled by open-crank v0.5.

  6. Track RRenode virtual-time driver

    Firmware in the loop in CI, without a board, in parallel. Pulled by open-crank's Track R.

  7. When a sibling starts

    open-robots in SIL, a Raspberry Pi Pico bridge and register-mapped slaves. They get a version number when open-robots starts; until then nothing is built.

open-crank ROADMAP.md

  1. v0.1Level 1 vertical slicedone

    Plant, engine ECU, J1939, portable controller and closed-loop tests; now on open-tick.

  2. v0.1.xMISRA-oriented checks, stack usagedone

    cppcheck's MISRA addon and -fstack-usage in CI. Practice, not compliance.

  3. v0.2Reference board on the bus

    An STM32G431 and a USB-CAN adapter run the portable controller unchanged.

  4. v0.2.1–v0.3Acceptance suite, robustness

    Grade any controller by its frames; fault injection, DM1 and a watchdog on the board.

  5. v0.4Level 2: your firmware as the engine ECU

    Crank and cam signals from open-tick's Tang Nano 20K bridge; fuel from your injector pulses.

  6. v0.5–v0.7Two engines, a marine two-stroke, power management

    Address claiming, a propeller load and a generator-set bus with a blackout scenario.

// how we work

Built to be trusted, so it is built to be checked.

Each principle is enforced by something that can fail: a test, a CI job, or a documented rule.

01

The plant answers honestly

Physics in the plant, decisions in a separate ECU. If the machine stays alive, your controller kept it alive.

02

Same source, laptop to board

Controller code builds for a Cortex-M4F on every push. A stray printf or double fails the build.

03

Tests that can fail

CI breaks behaviours on purpose and requires the tests to notice. Refactors must leave every trace identical, byte for byte.

04

Every number has a source

Parameters are marked as sourced, estimated or inherited. Representative, never presented as calibrated.

05

Generality is earned

open-tick was extracted only when a working machine needed it, and only once that machine's behaviour stayed exactly the same.

06

No claim stronger than the code

Anything that does not exist yet is marked planned, here and in every README.

// get involved

Pre-alpha, and the right moment to shape it.

A well-argued issue is worth more than a pull request right now.

Bring a controller

Point your own firmware at the simulated engine through open-crank's bus contract, on any board, RTOS or language, and tell us what broke.

Source a number

Bench access to a D936- or MAN B&W-class engine, or a copy of SAE J1939-71? Correct a parameter or a byte layout, and cite where it comes from.

Name the next machine

Cranes, robots, trains, generator sets: open an issue on open-tick with the machine you would practise on and the signals its controller sees.
